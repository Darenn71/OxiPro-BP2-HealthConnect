Get your OxiPro BP2 readings into Health Connect

The OxiPro BP2's own app doesn't connect to Health Connect. OxiPro Bridge connects to the monitor over Bluetooth, shows each reading, and saves it to Google Health Connect, where Google Fit, Samsung Health and other apps can see it.

How it works

Three taps from cuff to Health Connect.

1

Connect

Open the app and tap Connect & Sync. It finds your BP2 over Bluetooth and connects automatically.

2

Measure

Take your reading as normal. You'll see the cuff pressure live, then your result, colour-coded with a short plain-English summary.

3

Save

Tap Save to Health Connect. Blood pressure and pulse are saved with the time on your phone, ready for any app that reads Health Connect.

What the colours mean

Each number is checked against published reference ranges from the American Heart Association / American College of Cardiology and Cleveland Clinic.

Green

In the normal range, e.g. under 120/80 mmHg, or a pulse of 60–100 bpm.

Amber

Worth keeping an eye on, e.g. low blood pressure (under 90/60) or a pulse under 60.

Red

Above the normal range, e.g. 120/80 or higher, a pulse over 100, or an unusually narrow or wide pulse pressure.

What you need

  • An OxiPro BP2 Bluetooth blood pressure monitor.
  • An Android phone running Android 8.0 or newer, with Bluetooth.
  • Health Connect. It's built into Android 14 and newer. On older phones, install it free from the Google Play Store.

Questions

How do I install an APK?

Tap Download APK on your phone, then open the downloaded file. Android will ask you to allow installs from your browser or Files app the first time; allow it, then tap Install. Android may warn that the app isn't from the Play Store. That's expected for apps installed this way.

Do I still need the MedM Health Diary app?

No, but you can keep it. OxiPro Bridge works alongside it. It only handles new readings as you take them, so it doesn't import the history already stored on the monitor.

Where does my data go?

Only to Health Connect on your own phone, and only when you tap Save. The app has no account, no server and no analytics. The source code is public on GitHub if you'd like to check.

Why is the time different from the monitor's screen?

Readings are saved with your phone's clock, not the monitor's. If the BP2's own clock is wrong (for example after changing batteries), that only affects what the monitor itself displays.

Updating says "app not installed". What do I do?

The very first test builds were each signed differently. If you have one of those, uninstall it once and install the latest version. From then on, new versions install over the top. Anything you've already saved stays in Health Connect.

Does it work with other blood pressure monitors?

It's built for the OxiPro BP2's own Bluetooth protocol, so other models probably won't connect. The protocol notes are on GitHub if you'd like to adapt it.

Not medical advice. OxiPro Bridge shows general reference ranges, not a diagnosis, and isn't a medical device. If your readings are often outside the normal range, or you feel unwell, talk to a doctor. OxiPro Bridge is an independent project and isn't affiliated with or endorsed by OxiPro, MedM or Google.
